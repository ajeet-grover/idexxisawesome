## Insight Synthesis & Feedback

*Turn raw signal into themes. What pattern repeats?*

Source: raw NPS feedback, 10 responses, captured 2026-09-28.

- **The zero-reset kills motivation to continue, not just to recover the lost streak.**
  > "I hit a 20-day streak, missed one day, and it reset to zero. I haven't opened the app since. Felt pointless to start over."
  > "The streak is the only thing keeping me engaged, but the second I lost it, I was done."
  > "Deleted after 3 weeks. The moment I lost my streak the whole thing lost its meaning."

- **Users want a recovery path, and know competitors already offer one.**
  > "I broke my streak once and there was no way to recover it. Other apps let you freeze a streak. Why not this one?"
  > "I want Streakly to feel like a coach that helps me get back on track, not a scorekeeper that punishes me for missing a day."
  > "I wish it would make coming back easier instead of making me feel like I failed and have to start from scratch."

- **The app gives no acknowledgment of where a user actually is.**
  > "The home screen looks the same whether I'm on a 2-day streak or coming back after two weeks away. Nothing acknowledges where I am."

- **Notifications are frequent/random, not personalized — and that's driving opt-out, not re-engagement.**
  > "The notifications feel random. I got three in one afternoon and just turned them all off."
  > "The first week was genuinely fun. After that the daily reminder just started to feel like nagging."

- **New tension not previously captured: users who drift away (rather than break a streak) say they'd respond to a useful pull-back, suggesting the fix isn't just fewer/softer notifications but more relevant ones.**
  > "Love the lessons. I just forget it exists after a couple of days. If it pulled me back with something useful I'd come back."

This corroborates the existing hypothesis in [4strategy.md](../01-orient/4strategy.md) (all-or-nothing reset + no recovery path driving churn) and adds two points not yet reflected there: (1) notification complaints are about volume/randomness, not just tone, and (2) some disengaged users aren't reacting to a *break* at all — they're drifting from inactivity and want a relevant nudge back, which is closer to a re-engagement problem than a comeback-from-failure problem.

## Competitive Matrix

Full profiles, comparison table, and sources in [competitive-matrix.md](competitive-matrix.md). Summary:

| Competitor | Strength | Weakness | Implication |
|------------|----------|----------|-------------|
| Duolingo | Habit-forming gamification at mass-market scale | New "Energy" system made lapses more punitive, causing a visible uninstall wave | Punitive tightening measurably backfires — evidence for the opposite move |
| Babbel | Low-pressure, content-first approach avoids the punishment problem entirely | No strong daily-return mechanic to bring lapsed users back | Removing pressure isn't enough on its own — needs an active pull, not just less push |
| Elevate | Freezes are automatic and forgiving by default | Freeze is proactive-only; no recovery flow once a streak is actually broken | Confirms the gap: nobody softens the *moment after* a break |
| Brilliant | AI tutor (Koji) personalizes what to learn next | Personalization is about content, not about re-engaging after a lapse | Personalized recovery (not just content) is unclaimed |
| Streaks | Frictionless logging reduces misses in the first place | No content or recovery flow at all — pure tracking | Prevention-only; doesn't address what to do once a miss happens |

**White space for Streakly:** (1) no competitor offers a real post-break comeback experience, only pre-break protection; (2) no competitor personalizes the response to *why* a streak broke. Detail in [competitive-matrix.md](competitive-matrix.md).
